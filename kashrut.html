Why kashrut comes first

At BASAR, kashrut is the foundation of everything we do. We serve families who will not compromise on the halachic standard of the meat they bring home — and neither will we. Every product in our shop is prepared according to the most stringent opinions, so that you can serve it at your table with complete peace of mind.

Glatt Beit Yosef

After shechita, the lungs of every animal are carefully examined (bedika). Many standards permit an animal whose lungs had certain adhesions (sirchot) that were removed. The ruling of Maran, the Beit Yosef — Rabbi Yosef Karo, author of the Shulchan Aruch — is far stricter: only an animal whose lungs are completely smooth, with no adhesions at all, is acceptable.

This is Glatt Beit Yosef (חלק בית יוסף), the standard followed by Sephardic communities and by all who wish to be stringent. It is the only standard of meat we sell.

“Only an animal whose lungs are entirely smooth — the ruling of the Beit Yosef — reaches our counter.”

Sephardic nikkur achoraim

The Torah forbids eating certain fats (chelev) and the sciatic nerve (gid hanasheh), most of which are found in the hindquarter of the animal. Removing them — nikkur, or porging — is painstaking work that demands deep halachic knowledge and great skill.

Because it is so demanding, many kosher butchers outside of Israel simply do not sell the hindquarter at all. At BASAR, the hindquarter is porged by expert menakrim according to the custom of the Sephardic communities (ניקור אחורי כמנהג הספרדים). This is what allows us to offer cuts that are rarely available kosher:

  • Filet mignon and whole beef tenderloin
  • New York strip / striploin
  • Picanha and rump steak
  • Leg of lamb, lamb tenderloin and lamb sirloin

Under the supervision of Badatz Mekor Chaim

Every stage of the process — shechita, bedika, nikkur, soaking and salting, cutting and packing — is carried out under the supervision of Badatz Mekor Chaim (בד״ץ מקור חיים). Our meat arrives soaked and salted (kashered) according to halacha and is sealed and labeled under supervision.

A note on liver: by halacha, liver cannot be kashered by salting and must be kashered by broiling over an open flame. Please ask us about liver that has already been kashered.
